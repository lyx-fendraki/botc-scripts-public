  1. Sprawl Requiem

    by Lyx

    Seoul doesn't end, it just gets louder. Above, the arcology spires crowd a sky the chaebols sold off decades ago - a ceiling of neon and ad-glare that never lets the stars through. Below, past the last flickering signboard, the Barrens sprawl on forever: rust and monsoon rain and the blue flicker of a trideo no one's left alive to watch. This is the Sixth World, chummer. Magic came back, and it didn't bring wonder - it brought ghouls in the alleys, spirits wearing the dead, and a Matrix that knows your name, your debts, and your dreams better than you do.

    Down here a SIN is a leash, and the lack of one is a death sentence. Everything you are is for sale or already repossessed: your chrome, your memories, the face you wear to the people who think they know you. Trust is a luxury nobody in the sprawl can afford, because nothing is what it claims to be - the fixer vouching for the team, the medic with the platinum contract, the quiet runner in the corner who hasn't said a word all night. One of them is lying. Maybe all of them.

    And lately the bodies in the Barrens don't stay down. Something is loose in the lower grid - something patient and hungry that the corps won't name and the cameras never log. The job was supposed to be simple. The job is never simple. The sun won't be up for hours, the handshake's already corrupted, and one of the faces around this table isn't a face at all. Jack in, watch your back, and pray to whatever spirit still answers down here. Not everyone makes it to dawn, chummer. Most nights, nobody's meant to.

  2. Talismonger

    On your 1st night, choose a player (not a Traveller): you learn 1 good & 1 evil character, 1 of which is correct. When they die, you become the other.

    “Two futures on the table, chummer. One's a lie. You won't know which until the body drops.”

    The Talismonger becomes 1 of 2 characters once their chosen player dies.

    How to Run

    On the first night, show 1 good & 1 evil character token and put the GOOD or EVIL reminder by the chosen player, marking which of the two is correct. When that player dies, wake the Talismonger and show them which character they have become, swap their character token for it, and mark them with IS THE TALISMONGER, so you still know who they truly are.

    • Although the Talismonger may turn into an evil character, they are still a good player.
    • If the Talismonger is drunk or poisoned on their first night, no character was ever validly marked correct: if their chosen player later dies while the Talismonger is sober and healthy, they become the evil character.
    • If the Talismonger is drunk or poisoned at the moment their chosen player dies, the swap is wasted - they remain the Talismonger, with no ability.

  3. Rigger

    On your 1st night, choose a player (not a Traveller): you learn how many character types there are amongst them & their neighbours (except Travellers).

    “My drones see the block better than God does. Three heat signatures - and two of them are wrong.”

    The Rigger learns how mixed one stretch of the circle is.

    How to Run

    • The types that count are Townsfolk, Outsider, Minion and Demon, so the answer is between 1 and 3.
    • Travellers stay out of it entirely: they cannot be chosen, and if one sits next to the chosen player, the next player beyond them counts as the neighbour instead.
    • Neighbours here include the dead - only Travellers are skipped.

    Examples

    • The Rigger scans a Townsfolk whose neighbours are a Minion and the Demon. The Rigger learns a '3'.
    • The Rigger scans themselves; their neighbours are a Decker and the SINless (registering as a Townsfolk this scan). The Rigger learns a '1'.

    Townsfolk

  4. Face

    Each night*, you learn if you & the Demon both voted on today's 1st nomination.

    “I read a room better than any datajack. When the dead thing leans on a vote, I feel the whole table tilt with it.”

    The Face knows if the Demon voted alongside them on the day's 1st nomination.

    How to Run

    As soon as the day's 1st nomination has been counted, mark the Face with VOTED TOGETHER or VOTED APART. That night, answer accordingly, then remove the reminder.

    • Both of them must have voted. If the Face did not raise their hand, the answer is NO even if the Demon voted - and if there was no nomination today, it is NO as well.
    • A vote counts whether or not the nominee was executed.
    • If the Shedim is in play, the Demon is whoever was the host that day.

    Examples

    • The Face lets the 1st nomination pass, then votes on 3 later ones - the Demon voted on 2 of them. That night, the Face still learns NO.

    Townsfolk

  5. Decker

    Each night*, choose a player: you learn a reminder token next to their character token (if any).

    “Everyone leaves marks. I just read what's written under your skin.”

    The Decker reads what has been done to a player.

    How to Run

    • You choose which token to show if a player has several, and you show nothing at all if they have none.
    • Show the reminder token itself, not the character it belongs to.

    Townsfolk

  6. Shifter

    Each night*, choose an alive player. If good, they die & the Demon doesn't kill tonight. One good player registers as evil.

    “When the sun drops, so does the leash. I come to in an alley not knowing whose blood is on me - only that the things in the dark stayed home tonight.”

    The Shifter kills good players at night, and the Demon cannot kill when they do.

    How to Run

    During setup, mark 1 good player with FAUX PAW. While the Shifter lives, they register as evil to other abilities and the Shifter cannot kill them; their alignment and win condition are unchanged.

    • The Shifter must choose an alive player. If the chosen good player does not die for any reason, the Demon may still kill tonight.

  7. Bodyguard

    Each night*, choose a player (not yourself): they are safe from the Demon tonight.

    “Stay behind me and breathe easy, chummer. Whatever's hunting tonight goes through two tons of reflex-wired muscle first.”

    The Bodyguard protects other players from the Demon.

    How to Run

    Put the SAFE reminder by the chosen player and remove it at dawn.

    • This protects against the Demon only. Poison, an execution and kills from any other source get through.

  8. Sensitive

    Each night, choose a player who is not next to you: you learn if at least 1 of their alive neighbours is evil. There is a good player that registers as evil to you.

    “I don't read minds. I just feel the cold coming off the ones who've stopped being people.”

    The Sensitive detects evil beside a chosen player, but 1 good player always registers as evil to them.

    How to Run

    During setup, mark 1 good player with FALSE PULSE. They register as evil to the Sensitive for the whole game; their own alignment is unchanged.

    • The answer is a plain yes or no, never a count.
    • The Sensitive may choose themselves, but not a player whose alive neighbours would include the Sensitive - the 2 players being checked are always others. Watch for this late in the game, when the players in between have died.

    Townsfolk

  9. Analyst

    Each night, you learn how many players' abilities worked abnormally (since dawn) due to another character's ability.

    “The numbers don't lie - but tonight they don't add up. Somebody's running a cheat.”

    The Analyst knows how many players' abilities are not working correctly.

    How to Run

    Whenever an ability malfunctions because of another character, mark that player with ABNORMAL. Give the count at night, then clear the reminders at dawn.

    • Only interference from another character counts. A character's own built-in falsehood does not: the Seer's red herring and the Sensitive's false pulse are part of their own ability and never raise the number.

    Townsfolk

  10. Snoop

    Each night, you learn a character that is not in play.

    “Half of legwork is finding out who never showed. Somebody is always booked somewhere else tonight - and that is a name you get to cross off.”

    The Snoop learns, one by one, which characters are not in play.

    How to Run

    • You choose which character to show, and that choice is the dial: a not-in-play Outsider or Minion narrows the game far more than one more Townsfolk.
    • Keep a note of what you have shown. Repeating a character is allowed, but rarely worth it.
    • A character a player only thinks they are is not in play and may be shown: the Ghoul's Townsfolk, the Latent's Outsider mask, and the Townsfolk ability an awakened Latent holds. These are true answers that point straight at a hidden role, so save them for later.
    • Once a character genuinely enters play through a Talismonger swap or the Invae's trade, stop showing it.
    • The Snoop is slow but relentless: every night takes one bluff off the board.
    • It is a comfortable Demon bluff as well - nothing a Snoop claims can be checked by anyone except the Storyteller.

    Townsfolk

  11. Detox

    Each night, choose a player: they are protected from being drunk or poisoned until dusk.

    “Pop the patch, ride out the shakes, walk it off. Whatever they slipped into your system, I can flush it - once a night, for one of us.”

    The Detox keeps 1 player clear of drunkenness and poison each night.

    How to Run

    Put the PROTECTED reminder by the chosen player and remove it at dusk.

    • The Detox blocks any source of poison or drunkenness for that player until dusk, including the Ghoul's neighbour aura - but only for that 1 player.

  12. Seer

    Each night, choose 2 players: you learn if either is a Demon. There is a good player that registers as a Demon to you.

    “The aura reading's mostly noise down here - but every so often, something stares back that shouldn't.”

    The Seer detects Demons, but 1 good player always registers as a Demon to them.

    How to Run

    During setup, mark 1 good player with RED HERRING. They register as a Demon to the Seer for the whole game; their own alignment is unchanged.

    Townsfolk

  13. Fixer

    Each day, you may publicly invite 1 alive player seated next to you or a crew member to join your crew. Tonight, if an evil player joined, the entire crew dies.

    “I built crews for twenty years. Every name I ever vouched for is a debt - and the wrong name burns us all.”

    The Fixer builds a growing bloc of publicly vouched-for players.

    How to Run

    Put a CREW reminder by the Fixer during setup, and another by each player who accepts an invitation. If an evil player joined today, every living crew member dies that night.

    • Each day the Fixer may invite at most 1 player; invitation and answer are public, and joining is voluntary.
    • "Seated next to" skips the dead: a player borders the crew if no living player sits between them and a member. Membership is permanent - dead members still anchor further invitations, but do not die again.
    • Never intervene. Do not correct an invitation that does not border the crew, and do not confirm one that does; seating and membership are public. Any player may claim to be the Fixer and hand out invitations - nothing happens, exactly as with a poisoned Fixer.
    • The trigger is the act of joining, not a member's alignment later on (Mr. Johnson, Invae, Black Widow).
    • Check the Fixer's state at night, not during the day. If they are drunk or poisoned then, nothing happens and the trigger is spent: the crew keeps an evil member who now looks verified to the whole town. A second evil player joining later triggers normally.
    • The crew dies at night, not by execution: the Bodyguard does not prevent it, and execution-triggered abilities (Invae, the Ghilani's infection) do not fire.

  14. Dzoo-noo-qua

    You have the ability of the recently killed executee. If they are evil, you are poisoned until a good player dies by execution.

    “Waste not. The dead don't need their tricks anymore - just hope the last meal wasn't rotten.”

    The Dzoo-noo-qua gains the ability of the last player to be executed.

    How to Run

    Each time a player is executed and dies, move the LUNCH reminder to them. If they were good, remove the Dzoo-noo-qua's POISONED reminder; if they were evil, put it there. Never say which ability was gained.

    • The Dzoo-noo-qua wakes whenever the gained character would wake.
    • While poisoned, wake them as if the evil player's bluffed character had been gained, and give false information.
    • A player must be executed and die: executing a dead player, or one who does not die, grants nothing.

    Townsfolk

  15. Adept

    Once per game, at night, choose a good character: gain that ability. If this character is in play, they are drunk.

    “The talent's already out there in the mana - you just reach in and take the shape the run is missing. Tonight I wake up as someone useful.”

    The Adept takes a good character's ability once, and keeps it for the rest of the game.

    How to Run

    When the Adept chooses, swap their character token for the chosen one, mark them with IS THE ADEPT so you still know who they truly are, and put HAS ABILITY by them. If that character is in play, put DRUNK by that player.

    • The Adept has no ability until they choose, and may wait as many nights as they like.
    • They may take any good character, Townsfolk or Outsider, never an evil one.
    • The drunk player stays drunk as long as the Adept holds the ability. If the Adept dies, or becomes drunk or poisoned, they sober up.
    • If the gained ability works at night, the Adept wakes when that character would. If it is a first-night-only ability, they use it the night they choose.
    • If the gained ability works while dead, it works while the Adept is dead.

    Townsfolk

  16. Street Samurai

    Once per game, during the day, publicly choose a player: if they are evil, they die.

    “Mil-spec reflexes, a smartgun link, and one round chambered for the thing in the room that shouldn't be breathing. I only ever need the one.”

    The Street Samurai can kill an evil player by naming them in public.

    How to Run

    Once the Street Samurai has used their ability, put NO ABILITY by them - whether or not anyone died.

    • They kill any player who registers as evil, not only the Demon. A good player who registers as evil might be killed, and an evil player who registers as good might survive.

    Jinxes

    The Street Samurai cannot kill the player with the Faux Paw.

    Townsfolk

  17. Latent

    You think you are an Outsider, but you are not. The 1st time an evil player chooses you with their ability, it fails & you gain a not-in-play Townsfolk ability. [+0 or +1 Outsider]

    “They ran every scan money could buy and found nothing - just chrome and static, they said. Then a Ghilani cyst tried to bloom behind my eyes, and something older than the corp that built me finally woke up to stop it.”

    The Latent stops the first evil ability that picks them and gains a Townsfolk ability from it. [+0 or +1 Outsider]

    How to Run

    During setup, decide whether the game has its normal number of Outsiders or 1 extra. Before the tokens go in the bag, remove the Latent token and add an Outsider that is not in play. Put IS THE LATENT by whoever draws it while preparing the first night: they are the Latent from now on, but think they are that Outsider. Note privately which not-in-play Townsfolk ability they will awaken with. The reminder stays for the rest of the game - after the later token swap it is your only record of who this player truly is.

    The first time an evil player's ability chooses the Latent, wake them where that ability sits in the night order, show the YOU ARE token and the Townsfolk token you noted, and swap their character token for it.

    • Neither the mask nor the noted Townsfolk may be handed out as a Demon bluff.
    • The mask must be an Outsider that is not in play, so at most 4 of this script's 5 Outsiders can be in play alongside them. If the other setup modifiers already bring the count to 4, choose +0.
    • The triggering ability has no effect at all beyond waking the Latent: it does not poison, kill, protect or redirect anything.
    • The Latent may use the gained ability that same night.
    • The Townsfolk whose ability they hold is still not in play - the swapped token is only your record. The Snoop may still learn that character.
    • This happens only once. If the Latent is chosen again afterwards, that ability works on them normally.

    Examples

    • The Nuyen spends 2 nuyen to poison the Latent, thinking they've neutralized a harmless Outsider. The poison fails to take hold - being chosen is enough to wake the Latent, who instantly gains the Sensitive's ability, a real ability from then on.
    • Spoof Command names the Latent as its decoy, meaning to redirect the Bodyguard's protection through them. The redirection fails, and the Bodyguard's protection stays on its original target. Being chosen this way is enough to wake the Latent, who holds the Rigger's ability for the rest of the game.
    • The Latent is a strong, quiet insurance policy: it looks like a wasted Outsider slot until an evil player's own move on them backfires into a fresh Townsfolk ability.
    • Since the awakened ability belongs to a character that is not in play, no other player can honestly claim it - what the Latent brings to the table is unique.

    Townsfolk

  18. Ghoul

    You think you are a Townsfolk & gain their ability. Players seated next to you are poisoned. If you are “mad” about being the Ghoul, you might die tonight.

    “Don't look at me like that. I pay my rent, I keep my SIN clean. The hunger's just... a condition.”

    The Ghoul poisons the players to either side of them without knowing it.

    How to Run

    During setup, remove the Ghoul token and add a Townsfolk that is not in play. Mark whoever draws it with IS THE GHOUL and put a POISONED reminder by each of the 2 players seated next to them; both stay there for the whole game.

    • The Ghoul truly is that Townsfolk and their ability genuinely works: it triggers as normal and the information they get is true. The Ghoul is not drunk or poisoned by their own ability.
    • Poisoned are the 2 players seated next to them, not their living neighbours: a dead player is poisoned as well and shields the player beyond them.
    • "You might die tonight" is your choice: if the Ghoul claims or hints that they are the Ghoul, you may kill them that night, but you do not have to.

  19. SINless

    Evil knows you are the SINless, the Storyteller may lie to you & you can register as any character.

    “No SIN, no record, no face the cameras keep. I was never here. I was never anyone.”

    The SINless is shown to the evil team, may be lied to, and can register as any character.

    How to Run

    • Show the SINless to the evil team on the first night.
    • Which character the SINless registers as is your choice, and it may differ from one ability to the next.
    • Lie where the SINless can act on it. Once the table believes nothing they say, the lies cost nothing.

    Examples

    • The Storyteller tells the SINless that a hot deck just made them a Decker. When the SINless "jacks in" to use it, the ST says "Nothing happens" - and the table watches them flail.
    • The Storyteller tells the SINless that the Shedim has chosen them, even if no Shedim is in play.
    • Since evil always knows who the SINless is, they also know when the SINless is not in play - which can be useful for bluffing.

    Jinxes

    The SINless registers as the SINless to Convergence.

  20. Loose End

    When you die, the Demon chooses a player: they learn that player's character.

    “Every runner's a loose end to somebody. When they finally snip me, they'll read whatever I was still carrying.”

    The Loose End hands the Demon a last piece of information when it dies.

    How to Run

    When the Loose End dies, mark them with DEAD. That night, the Demon chooses a player and you show the Demon that player's character token; then remove the reminder.

    • If the Loose End dies at night, this is resolved the same night, after the kills. If they die by day, it waits for that night.

    Outsider

  21. Otaku

    Drunk or poisoned Townsfolk abilities yield false info. When you die, 1 player is drunk from now on.

    “I never needed a deck - the grid just let me in and never fully let go. Get loose enough in the head near me, and the static bleeds through as gospel.”

    The Otaku denies drunk and poisoned Townsfolk any chance of accidentally true information.

    How to Run

    When the Otaku dies, choose a player and put the DRUNK reminder by their character; they are drunk for the rest of the game. You may choose any player, including an evil one.

    • While the Otaku is alive, apply this whenever a drunk or poisoned Townsfolk's ability would normally have a chance of returning true information: give them false information instead, guaranteed.
    • What matters is the player's character type, not where their ability came from. Outsiders, Minions and the Demon are unaffected even while the Otaku lives - including the Ghoul, who is an Outsider despite having a Townsfolk ability.
    • The effect ends the moment the Otaku dies. The player they leave drunk is a normal drunk player, whose information may occasionally be true.
    • The Otaku makes a strong Demon bluff. Nothing about the claim can be checked while they live, and the threat of leaving a permanent drunk behind gives the town a reason not to execute them.
    • Killing the Otaku is rarely urgent for the Demon: their death costs good a Townsfolk's information for the rest of the game.

    Outsider

  22. Invae

    When you are executed, that night the Demon chooses an alive good player: they become an evil Minion, or refuse & die, even if for some reason they could not. They learn which Minion only if they accept.

    “I never felt the eggs take. When I die, the hive won't - it just needs someone new to hatch into.”

    The Invae turns its own execution into a recruitment offer.

    How to Run

    When the Invae is executed, mark them with HATCHING. That night the Demon chooses an alive good player and you choose which Minion is offered. Wake that player and put the offer to them: if they accept, show the YOU ARE and Minion tokens & give a thumbs-down, swap their character token and mark them NEW MINION; if they refuse, they die - mark them DEAD. Remove HATCHING afterwards.

    • A player who accepts really does change sides: they are that Minion, they are evil, and they win with the evil team.
    • Only an execution triggers the Invae. Dying at night or by any other means does nothing.
    • Usually a Minion that is not in play, but as with the Plague Doctor you may duplicate one already in play - pick to suit the game state, weak if evil is ahead, sharp if they are behind.
    • The chosen player learns nothing about which character it is until they accept. If they refuse, the death cannot be prevented in any way, so the Demon can rely on it.
    • They do not learn who the Demon is - like the Mezepheles or Fang Gu conversions, becoming evil grants no automatic team info.

    Outsider

  23. Toxic Shaman

    Each night, choose a player: they are poisoned tonight and tomorrow day.

    “The city's already poisoned, chummer. I just decide who gets the dose tonight.”

    The Toxic Shaman secretly disrupts character abilities.

    How to Run

    Each night, put the POISONED reminder by the chosen player's character token. Remove it at the next dusk, when the poison runs out.

    • If a poisoned player uses a "once per game" ability while poisoned, they cannot use it again.

  24. Mr. Johnson

    On your 1st night, choose a player. If there are 5 or more players alive & you die, they turn evil that night & learn you are Mr. Johnson. (Travellers don't count.) [-1 or +1 Outsider]

    “You'll never meet me twice with the same face. And when I'm gone, one of you is already mine.”

    Mr. Johnson leaves a recruit behind when they die.

    How to Run

    On the first night, put the RECRUITED reminder by the chosen player. If Mr. Johnson dies while 5 or more players are alive, wake that player that night: show the YOU ARE token & a thumbs down, then the Mr. Johnson token, then point to Mr. Johnson. Remove the reminder afterwards.

    • Count the players alive immediately before Mr. Johnson dies, counting Mr. Johnson himself.
    • At 4 or fewer, nothing happens and the reminder comes off.
    • Turning evil changes neither their character nor their ability - they keep both and now win with the evil team.
    • Decide at setup whether the game has 1 more or 1 fewer Outsider than normal.

    Minion

  25. Spoof Command

    Each night, choose 2 players: tonight, everyone who chooses the 1st with their ability chooses the 2nd instead.

    “Their probe pings exactly where I tell it to. The data comes back clean, true, and pointed at the wrong soul.”

    The Spoof Command turns 1 player into a decoy for every ability aimed at them.

    How to Run

    Each night, put DECOY by the 1st chosen player and TARGET by the 2nd. Both come off at dawn.

    • Every ability counts, good or evil, including the Demon's kill: if the Demon chooses the decoy, the target dies instead.
    • If an ability chooses several players and the decoy is among them, only the decoy is swapped - the other choices stand.
    • If the redirection would give a player an illegal target, prompt them to choose again (Bodyguard, Sensitive, Ghilani). Do not wake a player at all if the decoy is their only legal choice.
    • Only the choice itself is redirected, not what follows from it. The Rigger and the Sensitive read the neighbours of whoever they choose: if they choose the decoy, the target's neighbours are read instead - but if the decoy merely sits next to the chosen player, nothing changes.
    • An ability that chooses no players is unaffected.
    • Players never learn that their choice was redirected. Give them true information about the target, exactly as if they had chosen them.

    Minion

  26. SURGE

    You do not know your ability. Each night, choose whether to activate it.

    “I don't know what I'll set off when I trigger. Neither do you. That's exactly why they keep me on the payroll.”

    SURGE has an ability that only the Storyteller knows.

    How to Run

    Decide SURGE's entire ability before the first night; it does not change for the rest of the game. Use the ? reminders for whatever that ability needs to track.

    • The ability must harm the good team. Depending on what you pick, it may hit the evil team as well.
    • Tailor it to the setup: it may be as strong as any other Minion ability, including a once-per-game one like the Assassin's.
    • SURGE only ever learns whether they activated it, never what it does.

    Minion

  27. Convergence

    Each night*, guess the character of every other player. The 1st time every guess is correct, play for 1 more day: if the Demon is not executed that day, your team wins.

    “Every illegal ping you make raises the score. You never see the number climb - only the black helos that drop in when it tops out.”

    Convergence wins the game by naming every character correctly.

    How to Run

    The first time every guess is correct, put CONVERGED in the centre of the Grimoire and announce publicly that only 1 more day is played.

    • On the extra day, good wins only by executing the Demon. Executing a Black Widow while 5 or more players are alive hands the role on instead of killing her - the Demon was not executed, so evil wins.

    Minion

  28. Nuyen

    Each night, you get 1 nuyen & may earn more by completing the Storyteller's objectives. Each night, you may spend nuyen: 2 to poison a player, 3 to learn a player's character, or 4 to choose a player who dies.

    “Everything's got a price, chummer - a vote, a secret, a body in the alley. I just keep the ledger and collect.”

    The Nuyen pays for each of its effects with money it earns night by night.

    How to Run

    Track the balance with the numbered reminders 1-5: whenever it changes, swap to the token showing the new total. The Nuyen starts on 0 with no token at all and never holds more than 5. Mark a purchased poison with POISONED and a purchased kill with DEAD.

    Each night, first give them any nuyen for a completed objective, then 1 more, and show them the balance. Set a new objective if you wish. Then they may spend: they point at a player and pay 2 to poison them until dusk, 3 to be shown their character token, or 4 to have them die tonight.

    • A player killed by the Nuyen dies as a normal night kill.
    • Apply a purchased poison before waking the information roles, so that it takes effect.
    • Set objectives that fit the situation and require active, public play, never something passive. Pay 1 nuyen for a low-risk objective and 2 for a riskier one; no objective is worth more.
    • You may adjust the costs.

    Examples

    Sample objectives the Storyteller can assign (keep secret, scale to risk):

    1 nuyen:

    • Vote along when a good player is executed.
    • Be nominated yourself and survive the day.

    2 nuyen:

    • Nominate a good player who is then executed.
    • Vote the Demon off a nomination they were genuinely facing execution on, so they survive.

    Minion

  29. Shedim

    Each night, Minions choose a player as the Shedim's host: they are evil, “the Demon” & have no ability. Good players die when the Shedim leaves them. [+1 Minion]

    “The body was empty when I found it. They always are, down here. I just moved in.”

    The Shedim is not a player: it moves into a new host every night.

    How to Run

    During setup, remove the Shedim token and add a Minion token instead, and skip Minion Info and Demon Info on the first night. Each night the Minions choose the host - move the HOST reminder to that player. If the previous host was good, they die.

    • Dead Minions still wake and choose. If they choose a dead player, the Demon "is dead" and the game ends.
    • The host plays for the evil team until the next night and loses with evil if executed. They have no ability of their own while hosting.
    • The previous host's death is a kill by the Demon, so the Bodyguard and the Shifter can prevent it.
    • The host registers as the Demon: if they die, the game is over and good has won.

    If the Street Samurai chooses the Shedim host, the host dies.

  30. Black Widow

    Each night*, choose a player: they die. On the 1st night, 1 good player learns you are the Black Widow. If you die while 5 or more players are alive, they are evil & the Black Widow from now on. [-1 Outsider]

    “They all think they're the one I won't kill. One of them is right - for now.”

    The Black Widow passes her role on to the player who knows her when she dies.

    How to Run

    On the first night, choose which good player knows the Black Widow and put KNOW by them. If she dies while 5 or more players are alive, wake that player: show the YOU ARE and Black Widow tokens & give a thumbs-down, then swap their character token. Remove KNOW and put ONCE in the centre of the Grimoire - from then on she kills like any Demon, with nobody knowing her and no further transfer.

    • Count the players alive immediately before she dies, counting the Black Widow herself. Travellers do not count.
    • At fewer than 5 there is no transfer: the game ends as normal and the player who knows her stays good.
    • The Black Widow may choose herself with her night kill and hand the role over deliberately. She is then dead and evil, and wins or loses with her team.
    • If the player who knows her is already dead when she dies, they still turn evil and become the Black Widow - but a dead player has no ability, so no Demon is alive and good wins. They lose with the evil team.

    Jinxes

    If the Talismonger is chosen as the player who knows the Black Widow, they are never shown Convergence as their evil character.

  31. Killswitch

    Each night*, choose a player: they die. On your 1st night, choose a player: they have a cortex bomb. When the bombed player dies, 1 of their alive neighbours dies too.

    “Somewhere in their skull sits a gram of shaped charge they never agreed to. The Killswitch doesn't need to find you - it just waits for someone to open the wrong door.”

    The Killswitch plants a bomb that takes a neighbour along when its carrier dies.

    How to Run

    On the first night, put CORTEX BOMB by the chosen player. The moment that player dies, 1 of their alive neighbours dies with them - straight away on an execution, and at night in the very moment of their death. Remove the reminder afterwards; the bomb is spent.

    • You choose which of the 2 alive neighbours dies. Alive neighbours are the nearest living player on each side, skipping the dead.
    • The explosion is a kill by the Demon, so the Bodyguard can protect against it.

  32. Ghilani

    Each night*, choose an infected player: they die. If no player is infected, choose a player: they are infected. Infected players are poisoned & infect their good alive, uninfected neighbours when they die.

    “Patient zero never feels the bite. By the time the sprawl notices the others turning, half the table is already carrying me.”

    The Ghilani only kills players its plague has already reached.

    How to Run

    Put an INFECTED reminder by every newly infected player; it stays for the rest of the game. On the first night, and on any night where nobody is infected, the Ghilani seeds the plague instead of killing.

    • Infected players are poisoned for as long as they live.
    • The infection skips the dead, the evil and the already infected: on each side it travels outward to the nearest player who is good, alive and not yet infected. If there is no such player on that side, nothing is infected there.
    • The Ghilani sees where the infection spreads, and may kill any infected player, not only the one it chose itself.
    • If the Ghilani is drunk or poisoned when an infected player dies, the infection does not spread - the spreading is their ability.
