  1. Sprawl Requiem

    by Lyx

    Seoul doesn't end, it just gets louder. Above, the arcology spires crowd a sky the chaebols sold off decades ago - a ceiling of neon and ad-glare that never lets the stars through. Below, past the last flickering signboard, the Barrens sprawl on forever: rust and monsoon rain and the blue flicker of a trideo no one's left alive to watch. This is the Sixth World, chummer. Magic came back, and it didn't bring wonder - it brought ghouls in the alleys, spirits wearing the dead, and a Matrix that knows your name, your debts, and your dreams better than you do.

    Down here a SIN is a leash, and the lack of one is a death sentence. Everything you are is for sale or already repossessed: your chrome, your memories, the face you wear to the people who think they know you. Trust is a luxury nobody in the sprawl can afford, because nothing is what it claims to be - the fixer vouching for the team, the medic with the platinum contract, the quiet runner in the corner who hasn't said a word all night. One of them is lying. Maybe all of them.

    And lately the bodies in the Barrens don't stay down. Something is loose in the lower grid - something patient and hungry that the corps won't name and the cameras never log. The job was supposed to be simple. The job is never simple. The sun won't be up for hours, the handshake's already corrupted, and one of the faces around this table isn't a face at all. Jack in, watch your back, and pray to whatever spirit still answers down here. Not everyone makes it to dawn, chummer. Most nights, nobody's meant to.

  2. Talismonger

    On your 1st night, choose a player (not a Traveller): you learn 1 good & 1 evil character, 1 of which is correct. When they die, you become the other.

    “Two futures on the table, chummer. One's a lie. You won't know which until the body drops.”

    On their first night the Talismonger chooses a non-Traveller player and learns one good and one evil character, exactly one of which is correct. When that chosen player dies, the Talismonger becomes the other character.

    How to Run

    • Although the Talismonger may turn into an evil character, they are still a good player.
    • If the Talismonger is drunk or poisoned on their first night, no character was ever validly marked correct. If their chosen player later dies while the Talismonger is sober and healthy, they become the evil character by default.
    • If the Talismonger is drunk or poisoned at the moment their chosen player dies (regardless of their state on the first night), the swap is wasted: they remain the Talismonger, with no ability.
    • Otherwise, the swap works normally: they become whichever character was not the correct one.
    • When they become the other character, swap their character token for it. Mark them with the IS THE TALISMONGER reminder to remember who they truly are.

    Townsfolk

  3. Rigger

    On your 1st night, choose a player (not a Traveller): you learn how many character types there are amongst them & their neighbours (except Travellers).

    “My drones see the block better than God does. Three heat signatures - and two of them are wrong.”

    On their first night the Rigger chooses a player who is not a Traveller and learns how many different character types (Townsfolk, Outsider, Minion, Demon) are present among that player and their two neighbours. Travellers stay out of it entirely: they cannot be chosen, and if one sits next to the chosen player, the next player beyond them counts as the neighbor instead.

    Examples

    • The Rigger scans a Townsfolk whose neighbours are a Minion and the Demon. The Rigger learns a '3'.
    • The Rigger scans themselves; their neighbours are a Decker and the SINless (registering as a Townsfolk this scan). The Rigger learns a '1'.
    • The character types that count here are Townsfolk, Outsider, Minion, and Demon. Travellers are never counted.
    • The highest number a Rigger may learn is a '3'.

    Townsfolk

  4. Face

    Each night*, you learn if you & the Demon both voted on today's 1st nomination.

    “I read a room better than any datajack. When the dead thing leans on a vote, I feel the whole table tilt with it.”

    The Face knows if the Demon voted alongside them on the day's 1st nomination.

    How to Run

    • Both of them must have voted on it. If the Face did not raise their hand, the answer is NO even if the Demon voted - and if there was no nomination today, it is NO as well.
    • A vote counts whether or not the nominee was executed.
    • If the Shedim is in play, the Demon is whoever was the host during that day.

    Examples

    • The Face lets the 1st nomination pass, then votes on 3 later ones - the Demon voted on 2 of them. That night, the Face still learns NO.

    Townsfolk

  5. Decker

    Each night*, choose a player: you learn a reminder token next to their character token (if any).

    “Everyone leaves marks. I just read what's written under your skin.”

    Each night (after the first) the Decker chooses a player and learns one reminder token currently placed beside that player's character token, if any - revealing what has been done to them.

    Townsfolk

  6. Shifter

    Each night*, choose an alive player. If good, they die & the Demon doesn't kill tonight. One good player registers as evil.

    “When the sun drops, so does the leash. I come to in an alley not knowing whose blood is on me - only that the things in the dark stayed home tonight.”

    Each night except the first, the Shifter chooses an alive player. If that player is good, they die and the Demon cannot kill that night; if that player is evil, nothing happens. While the Shifter lives, one good player registers as evil to other abilities and cannot be killed by the Shifter.

    How to Run

    • During setup, mark one good player with the Faux Paw reminder. While the Shifter lives, that player registers as evil to other abilities, but their alignment and win condition are unchanged, and the Shifter cannot kill them.
    • The Shifter must choose an alive player. If the chosen good player does not die for any reason, the Demon may still kill tonight.

    Townsfolk

  7. Bodyguard

    Each night*, choose a player (not yourself): they are safe from the Demon tonight.

    “Stay behind me and breathe easy, chummer. Whatever's hunting tonight goes through two tons of reflex-wired muscle first.”

    Each night (except the first) the Bodyguard guards a player other than themselves; that player is safe from the Demon's kill that night.

    Townsfolk

  8. Sensitive

    Each night, choose a player who is not next to you: you learn if at least 1 of their alive neighbours is evil. There is a good player that registers as evil to you.

    “I don't read minds. I just feel the cold coming off the ones who've stopped being people.”

    Each night the Sensitive chooses a player who is not seated next to them - they may even choose themselves - and learns whether at least one of that player's living neighbours is evil. The answer is a simple yes or no, not a count. One good player always reads as evil to the Sensitive (the false pulse).

    How to Run

    • The Sensitive may not choose a player whose living neighbours would include the Sensitive, so that the two players being checked are always others. Watch for this late in the game, when the players between them have died.

    Townsfolk

  9. Analyst

    Each night, you learn how many players' abilities worked abnormally (since dawn) due to another character's ability.

    “The numbers don't lie - but tonight they don't add up. Somebody's running a cheat.”

    Each night the Analyst learns how many players' abilities malfunctioned that day because of another character (poison, drunkenness, and the like).

    Townsfolk

  10. Snoop

    Each night, you learn a character that is not in play.

    “Half of legwork is finding out who never showed. Somebody is always booked somewhere else tonight - and that is a name you get to cross off.”

    The Snoop learns, one by one, which characters are not in play.

    How to Run

    • You choose which character to show, and that choice is the dial: a not-in-play Outsider or Minion narrows the game far more than one more Townsfolk.
    • Keep a note of what you have shown. Repeating a character is allowed, but rarely worth it.
    • A character a player only thinks they are is not in play and may be shown: the Ghoul's Townsfolk, the Latent's Outsider mask, and the Townsfolk ability an awakened Latent holds. These are true answers that point straight at a hidden role, so save them for later.
    • Once a character genuinely enters play (a Talismonger swap, the Invae's trade), stop showing it.
    • The Snoop is slow but relentless: every night takes one bluff off the board.
    • It is a comfortable Demon bluff as well - nothing a Snoop claims can be checked by anyone except the Storyteller.

    Townsfolk

  11. Detox

    Each night, choose a player: they are protected from being drunk or poisoned until dusk.

    “Pop the patch, ride out the shakes, walk it off. Whatever they slipped into your system, I can flush it - once a night, for one of us.”

    Each night, the Detox chooses a player; that player is protected from being drunk or poisoned until dusk.

    How to Run

    • The Detox blocks any source of poison or drunkenness for the chosen player until dusk, including the Ghoul's neighbour aura, but only for that one player.
    • Do not tell the Detox whether this prevented anything.

    Townsfolk

  12. Seer

    Each night, choose 2 players: you learn if either is a Demon. There is a good player that registers as a Demon to you.

    “The aura reading's mostly noise down here - but every so often, something stares back that shouldn't.”

    The Seer scans two players for the Demon's aura. One good player always reads as a Demon to the Seer (the red herring).

    Townsfolk

  13. Fixer

    Each day, you may publicly invite 1 alive player seated next to you or a crew member to join your crew. Tonight, if an evil player joined, the entire crew dies.

    “I built crews for twenty years. Every name I ever vouched for is a debt - and the wrong name burns us all.”

    Each day the Fixer may publicly invite an alive player seated next to the Fixer or a current crew member to join the crew, building a growing bloc. If an evil player joins, the entire crew dies that night.

    How to Run

    • The crew starts as just the Fixer. Each day they may invite at most 1 player; invitation and answer are both public, and joining is voluntary - a player may refuse.
    • "Seated next to" skips the dead: a player counts as seated next to a crew member if no living player sits between them. Membership is permanent, including after death - dead members still anchor further invitations, but only living members die when the crew is destroyed.
    • Never intervene. Do not correct an invitation that does not border the crew, and do not confirm one that does. Seating and crew membership are public, so the players can check this themselves. Any player may claim to be the Fixer and hand out invitations; nothing happens - exactly as with a poisoned Fixer.
    • What triggers the ability is the act of joining, not a member's alignment later on. If a crew member turns evil afterwards (Mr. Johnson, Invae, Black Widow), nothing happens.
    • Check the Fixer's state at night, not during the day. If the Fixer is drunk or poisoned at that moment, nothing happens - and the trigger is wasted. The crew stays intact with an evil player in it, who now looks verified to the whole town. A second evil player joining later triggers normally.
    • The crew dies at night; this is not an execution. Being safe from the Demon (Bodyguard) does not prevent it, and abilities keyed to execution (Invae, the Ghilani's infection) do not trigger.

    Townsfolk

  14. Dzoo-noo-qua

    You have the ability of the recently killed executee. If they are evil, you are poisoned until a good player dies by execution.

    “Waste not. The dead don't need their tricks anymore - just hope the last meal wasn't rotten.”

    The Dzoo-noo-qua has the ability of the most recently executed player. If that player was evil, the Dzoo-noo-qua is poisoned until a good player is executed.

    Townsfolk

  15. Adept

    Once per game, at night, choose a good character: gain that ability. If this character is in play, they are drunk.

    “The talent's already out there in the mana - you just reach in and take the shape the run is missing. Tonight I wake up as someone useful.”

    Once per game, at night, the Adept chooses a good character and gains that ability for the rest of the game. If that character is already in play, that player is drunk for as long as the Adept keeps the ability. The Adept has no ability until they choose, and may wait as many nights as they like.

    How to Run

    • The Adept gains the ability of any good character (Townsfolk or Outsider), never an evil one.
    • If necessary, swap the Adept's character token for the one they chose, and mark them with the IS THE ADEPT reminder to remember who they truly are.
    • If the chosen character is in play, that player is drunk while the Adept holds the ability. If the Adept later dies, or becomes drunk or poisoned, that player becomes sober again.
    • If the gained ability works at night, the Adept wakes when that character would. If it is a first-night-only ability, they use it the night they choose.
    • If the gained ability works while dead, it works while the Adept is dead.

    Townsfolk

  16. Street Samurai

    Once per game, during the day, publicly choose a player: if they are evil, they die.

    “Mil-spec reflexes, a smartgun link, and one round chambered for the thing in the room that shouldn't be breathing. I only ever need the one.”

    Once per game, during the day, the Street Samurai may publicly choose a player. If that player is evil, they die.

    How to Run

    • The Street Samurai kills any player who registers as evil, not only the Demon. A good player who registers as evil might be killed, and an evil player who registers as good might survive.
    • The choice is public, and the Street Samurai may only do this once per game, whether or not it kills anyone.

    Jinxes

    The Street Samurai cannot kill the player with the Faux Paw.

    Townsfolk

  17. Latent

    You think you are an Outsider, but you are not. The 1st time an evil player chooses you with their ability, it fails & you gain a not-in-play Townsfolk ability. [+0 or +1 Outsider]

    “They ran every scan money could buy and found nothing - just chrome and static, they said. Then a Ghilani cyst tried to bloom behind my eyes, and something older than the corp that built me finally woke up to stop it.”

    The Latent thinks they are an Outsider and has no ability at all - until an evil player's ability targets them. That ability fails, and the Latent gains a not-in-play Townsfolk ability instead. Like the Adept, they gain the ability without becoming that character. [+0 or +1 Outsider]

    How to Run

    • While setting up the game, choose whether the game has its normal number of Outsiders or one extra, then, before putting character tokens in the bag, remove the Latent token and add an Outsider character that is not in play instead. Put the Latent's IS THE LATENT reminder token in the Grimoire. While preparing the first night, put this reminder by whichever player drew that Outsider token, changing their character to the Latent; they are now a Townsfolk with no ability (but think they are that Outsider). Also privately note which not-in-play Townsfolk ability the Latent will awaken with, so that character cannot be given to anyone else either. Neither the mask nor that Townsfolk may be handed out as a Demon bluff.
    • The Latent's mask must be an Outsider that is not in play, so at most 4 of this script's 5 Outsiders can genuinely be in play alongside them. If the other setup modifiers already bring the count to 4, choose +0.
    • The first time any evil player's ability chooses the Latent as one of its targets, that ability has no effect at all beyond waking the Latent - it does not poison, kill, protect, or redirect anything. Wake the Latent at the point in the night order where that ability would normally wake - not earlier, not later. Show them the YOU ARE token, then the Townsfolk character token you noted at setup: they have that ability from now on and may use it that same night. Swap their character token for it in the Grimoire, exactly as you would for the Adept.
    • The Townsfolk whose ability they gained is still not in play - the swapped token is only your record of which ability they hold. The Snoop may still learn that character.
    • Keep the IS THE LATENT reminder in place for the rest of the game; do not remove it. Like the Adept's IS THE ADEPT reminder, it is your only record, after the token swap, of who this player truly is.
    • This can only happen once. If the Latent is chosen again afterwards, that ability works on them normally.

    Examples

    • The Nuyen spends 2 nuyen to poison the Latent, thinking they've neutralized a harmless Outsider. The poison fails to take hold - being chosen is enough to wake the Latent, who instantly gains the Sensitive's ability, a real ability from then on.
    • Spoof Command names the Latent as its decoy, meaning to redirect the Bodyguard's protection through them. The redirection fails, and the Bodyguard's protection stays on its original target. Being chosen this way is enough to wake the Latent, who holds the Rigger's ability for the rest of the game.
    • The Latent is a strong, quiet insurance policy: it looks like a wasted Outsider slot until an evil player's own move on them backfires into a fresh Townsfolk ability.
    • Since the awakened ability belongs to a character that is not in play, no other player can honestly claim it - what the Latent brings to the table is unique.

    Townsfolk

  18. Ghoul

    You think you are a Townsfolk & gain their ability. Players seated next to you are poisoned. If you are “mad” about being the Ghoul, you might die tonight.

    “Don't look at me like that. I pay my rent, I keep my SIN clean. The hunger's just... a condition.”

    The Ghoul believes they are a Townsfolk and really has that character's ability, but both players seated next to them are poisoned. If the Ghoul is made "mad" that they are the Ghoul, they might die that night.

    How to Run

    • The Ghoul truly is that Townsfolk and their ability genuinely works: it triggers as normal and the information they get is true. Nothing about it is faked, and the Ghoul is not drunk or poisoned by their own ability.
    • The Ghoul does not poison their living neighbours, only the players seated next to them.
    • "You might die tonight" is the Storyteller's choice: if the Ghoul claims or hints that they are the Ghoul, you may kill them that night, but you do not have to.

    Outsider

  19. SINless

    Evil knows you are the SINless, the Storyteller may lie to you & you can register as any character.

    “No SIN, no record, no face the cameras keep. I was never here. I was never anyone.”

    The SINless exists off every grid - no identity the Matrix will hold. Evil always knows who they are. The Storyteller may freely lie to the SINless, and the SINless may register as any Townsfolk, Outsider, Minion or Demon.

    Examples

    • The Storyteller tells the SINless that a hot deck just made them a Decker. When the SINless "jacks in" to use it, the ST says "Nothing happens" - and the table watches them flail.
    • The Storyteller tells the SINless that the Shedim has chosen them, even if no Shedim is in play.
    • The Storyteller tells the SINless that the Spoof Command has hijacked their feed; they spend all day chasing a target that was never really there.
    • Since Evil always knows who the SINless is, they also know when the SINless is not in play - which can be useful for bluffing.
    • ST note: use this sparingly. It is pure cruelty - and that is exactly the point.

    Outsider

  20. Loose End

    When you die, the Demon chooses a player: they learn that player's character.

    “Every runner's a loose end to somebody. When they finally snip me, they'll read whatever I was still carrying.”

    When the Loose End dies, by any means, the Demon chooses a player and learns that player's character.

    Outsider

  21. Otaku

    Drunk or poisoned Townsfolk abilities yield false info. When you die, 1 player is drunk from now on.

    “I never needed a deck - the grid just let me in and never fully let go. Get loose enough in the head near me, and the static bleeds through as gospel.”

    The Otaku is wired into the Matrix without ever having jacked in - an unstable, uncontrolled connection that leaks into anyone nearby whose senses are already compromised. While the Otaku lives, any Townsfolk who is drunk or poisoned always receives false information from their ability, removing the usual chance that it happens to be true. When the Otaku dies, that connection tears loose and lodges in a single player, who is drunk for the rest of the game.

    How to Run

    • While the Otaku is alive, apply this whenever a drunk or poisoned Townsfolk's ability would normally have a chance of returning true information: give them false information instead, guaranteed.
    • What matters is the player's character type, not where their ability came from. Outsiders, Minions and the Demon are unaffected even while the Otaku lives - including the Ghoul, who is an Outsider despite having a Townsfolk ability.
    • This effect ends the moment the Otaku dies. From then on, drunk and poisoned players get information as usual - your choice whether it happens to be true.
    • When the Otaku dies, choose a player and place the DRUNK reminder token by their character. That player is drunk for the rest of the game. You may choose any player, including an evil one, and they are not told.
    • The player the Otaku makes drunk is a normal drunk player: their information may occasionally be true, since the Otaku's other ability is no longer in effect.
    • The Otaku makes a strong Demon bluff. Nothing about the claim can be checked while they live, and the threat of leaving a permanent drunk behind gives the town a reason not to execute them.
    • Killing the Otaku is rarely urgent for the Demon: their death costs good a Townsfolk's information for the rest of the game.

    Outsider

  22. Invae

    When you are executed, that night the Demon chooses an alive good player: they become an evil Minion, or refuse & die, even if for some reason they could not. They learn which Minion only if they accept.

    “I never felt the eggs take. When I die, the hive won't - it just needs someone new to hatch into.”

    If the Invae is executed, the Demon secretly chooses a living good player that night and offers them a Minion character picked by the Storyteller. That player may accept, becoming that Minion and learning which one only once they agree, or refuse and die instead. A death at night does not trigger this.

    How to Run

    • Only an execution triggers the Invae. Dying at night or by any other means does nothing.
    • When the Invae is executed, mark them with the HATCHING reminder, resolve the exchange that night (see the night reminder), then remove the reminder.
    • The Demon chooses who is offered; you choose which Minion they would become. Usually a not-in-play Minion, but as with the Plague Doctor you may duplicate one already in play - pick to suit the game state, weak if evil is ahead, sharp if they are behind.
    • The chosen player learns nothing about which character it is until they accept. If they refuse, they die immediately instead - this death cannot be prevented in any way, so the Demon can rely on it.
    • If the player accepts, show them the YOU ARE token & the Minion character token, then swap their character token for it. They do not learn who the Demon is - like the Mezepheles or Fang Gu conversions, becoming evil grants no automatic team info.

  23. Mr. Johnson

    On your 1st night, choose a player. If you die, they turn evil that night & learn you are Mr. Johnson. If 5 or fewer players are alive, you lose this ability. [-1 or +1 Outsider]

    “You'll never meet me twice with the same face. And when I'm gone, one of you is already mine.”

    On their first night, Mr. Johnson secretly marks a player. If Mr. Johnson ever dies, that player turns evil that night and learns that Mr. Johnson recruited them. Once 5 or fewer players are alive, Mr. Johnson loses this ability, so dying then no longer recruits anyone. [-1 or +1 Outsider]

    Minion

  24. Spoof Command

    Each night, choose 2 players: tonight, everyone who chooses the 1st with their ability chooses the 2nd instead.

    “Their probe pings exactly where I tell it to. The data comes back clean, true, and pointed at the wrong soul.”

    Each night the Spoof Command forges one player's identity on the network: that player becomes a decoy, and everyone who chooses them with an ability tonight chooses the Spoof Command's target instead. The information that comes back is genuine, but about the wrong person. It affects every ability that chooses players: an information role can be aimed at an innocent, a protective role steered off the Demon's victim, and even the Demon's kill lands on the target.

    How to Run

    • Every ability counts, good or evil, including the Demon's kill: if the Demon chooses the decoy, the target dies instead.
    • If an ability chooses more than one player and the decoy is among them, only the decoy is swapped for the target. The other chosen players are unaffected.
    • Only the choice itself is redirected, not what follows from it. The Rigger and the Sensitive also read the neighbours of the player they choose: the Spoof Command applies only if the decoy was chosen directly, and then the target's neighbours are read instead. If the decoy merely sits next to the chosen player, nothing changes.
    • An ability that chooses no players is unaffected.
    • Players never learn that their choice was redirected. Give them true information about the target, exactly as if they had chosen them.

    Minion

  25. SURGE

    You do not know your ability. Each night, choose whether to activate it.

    “I don't know what I'll set off when I trigger. Neither do you. That's exactly why they keep me on the payroll.”

    Each night SURGE may activate their hidden ability. The Storyteller decides its effect; it always harms the good team and may also harm the evil team.

    How to Run

    • The Storyteller sets SURGE's ability at the start of the game; it does not change. SURGE never learns what it does.
    • It must be designed to harm the good team. It may also hit the evil team, depending on the ability.
    • The Storyteller tailors SURGE's ability to the setup; it may be as strong as any other Minion ability - even a once-per-game ability like the Assassin's, if the script allows.

    Minion

  26. Convergence

    Each night*, guess the character of every other player. The 1st time every guess is correct, play for 1 more day: if the Demon is not executed that day, your team wins.

    “Every illegal ping you make raises the score. You never see the number climb - only the black helos that drop in when it tops out.”

    Each night from its second onward, Convergence secretly guesses the character of every other player. The first time every guess is correct, the Storyteller publicly announces that only one more day is played: if the Demon is not executed that day, evil wins.

    How to Run

    • On the extra day, good wins only by executing the Demon. If a Black Widow is nominated and executed that day by the player who knows her, she transfers to that player instead of dying - the Demon was not executed, so evil wins.

    Minion

  27. Nuyen

    Each night, you get 1 nuyen & may earn more by completing the Storyteller's objectives. Each night, you may spend nuyen: 2 to poison a player, 3 to learn a player's character, or 4 to choose a player who dies.

    “Everything's got a price, chummer - a vote, a secret, a body in the alley. I just keep the ledger and collect.”

    Each night, the Nuyen gets 1 nuyen, to a maximum of 5. The Storyteller may also secretly set the Nuyen an objective; if they complete it, they get 1 or 2 more nuyen. Each night, the Nuyen may spend nuyen: 2 to poison a player until dusk, 3 to learn a player's character, or 4 to choose a player who dies that night. A player who dies this way dies as a normal night kill. The Storyteller may adjust these costs.

    How to Run

    Before the first night, the Nuyen has 0 nuyen (no reminder token yet). Track their balance with the numbered reminder tokens: swap to the token matching their new balance whenever it changes, to a maximum of 5.

    Each night, wake the Nuyen. First, give them any nuyen for an objective they completed. Then give them 1 nuyen and show them their balance. If you wish, secretly set them a new objective. Then they may spend nuyen: they point to a player and pay 2, and that player is poisoned until dusk; or pay 3, and you show them that player's character token; or pay 4, and that player dies tonight. Put the Nuyen to sleep.

    • A player killed by the Nuyen dies as a normal night kill.
    • Apply a purchased poison before waking the information roles, so that it takes effect.
    • Set objectives that fit the situation and require active, public play, never something passive. Pay 1 nuyen for a low-risk objective and 2 for a riskier one; no objective is worth more.
    • You may adjust the costs.

    Examples

    Sample objectives the Storyteller can assign (keep secret, scale to risk):

    1 nuyen:

    • Vote along when a good player is executed.
    • Be nominated yourself and survive the day.

    2 nuyen:

    • Nominate a good player who is then executed.
    • Vote the Demon off a nomination they were genuinely facing execution on, so they survive.

    Minion

  28. Shedim

    Each night, Minions choose an alive player as the Shedim's host: they are evil, “the Demon” & have no ability. Good players die when the Shedim leaves them. [+1 Minion]

    “The body was empty when I found it. They always are, down here. I just moved in.”

    The Shedim is not a player. Each night the Minions choose a player as its host: while they are the host, that player counts as evil and registers as the Demon and has no ability of their own. When the Shedim moves to a new host, the previous host dies if they are good. If the current host dies, good wins.

    How to Run

    During setup, remove the Shedim token and add a Minion token. On the first night, skip the Minion Info and Demon Info steps.

    Each night, wake all Minions: they point to an alive player, who is now the host. Dead Minions still wake and choose.

    Then wake the host and show them that they are now evil and "the Demon". They keep that role until the next night and play for the evil team - if they are executed, they lose with evil.

    If the previous host is good, they die. This death is a kill by the Demon, so the Bodyguard and the Shifter can prevent it. The host registers as the Demon: if they die, declare that the game is over and good has won.

    On the final day, at most 1 Minion should be alive, so the good team has a real chance to find the host.

    Jinxes

    If the Latent is chosen as the host before awakening, they awaken and do not die when the Shedim leaves them.

  29. Black Widow

    Each night*, choose a player: they die. 1 player knows you. If you die while 5 or more players are alive, they are evil & the Black Widow from now on. [-1 Outsider]

    “They all think they're the one I won't kill. One of them is right - for now.”

    Each night (after the first) the Black Widow kills a player. One player secretly knows who the Black Widow is. If the Black Widow dies while 5 or more players are alive, that player turns evil and becomes the new Black Widow instead of the game ending - but this can only happen once per game.

    How to Run

    • On the first night, choose which player knows the Black Widow. Any player may be chosen, but a good player makes for the more interesting game.
    • The transfer to a new Black Widow can only happen once per game. Once it happens, mark the ONCE reminder in the centre of the Grimoire: from then on, the Black Widow simply kills each night like a normal Demon, with no player who knows her and no further transfer possible.
    • If the Black Widow dies while fewer than 5 players are alive, there is no transfer - the game ends as normal and the player who knows her does not turn evil.
    • The Black Widow may choose herself with her night kill. While the player who knows her is alive and 5 or more players remain, this hands the role over deliberately; she is then dead and evil, and wins or loses with her team.
    • If the player who knows her is already dead when she dies, they still turn evil and become the Black Widow - but a dead player has no ability, so no Demon is alive and good wins. They lose with the evil team.

    Jinxes

    If the Talismonger is chosen as the player who knows the Black Widow, they are never shown Convergence as their evil character.

    Demon

  30. Killswitch

    Each night*, choose a player: they die. On your 1st night, choose a player: they have a cortex bomb. When the bombed player dies, 1 of their alive neighbours dies too.

    “Somewhere in their skull sits a gram of shaped charge they never agreed to. The Killswitch doesn't need to find you - it just waits for someone to open the wrong door.”

    On its first night, the Killswitch secretly plants a cortex bomb in a chosen player, who is never told. Each night after that, the Killswitch chooses a player, who dies, like any Demon. Whenever the bombed player dies - by execution or at night - one of their living neighbours dies too, and the bomb is then spent.

    Demon

  31. Ghilani

    Each night*, if no-one died today, choose a player: they die. On your 1st night, choose a player: they are infected. Infected players are poisoned & infect their good alive neighbours if executed. [+1 Outsider]

    “Patient zero never feels the bite. By the time the sprawl notices the others turning, half the table is already carrying me.”

    On its first night the Ghilani chooses a player: that player is infected and poisoned. Each night thereafter, if no one died that day, the Ghilani chooses a player: that player dies. When an infected player is executed, their two living good neighbours also become infected and are therefore poisoned. The infection spreads only through execution, never through a death at night.

    How to Run

    • Infected players are poisoned for as long as they live.
    • Only the execution of an infected player spreads the infection, and only to their living good neighbours. A night death does not spread it.
    • "If no one died today" counts any death that day - an execution or a daytime kill. If someone died that day, the Ghilani does not kill that night.
